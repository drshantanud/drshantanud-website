Nolen Gur Sandesh, Lighter

DessertVegetarianGood sourceDS3

Kolkata's winter sweet: fresh chhena kneaded with nolen gur until smooth, then cooked briefly and shaped. This version uses less gur and no added sugar.

Region
West Bengal (Kolkata)
Status

Ingredients

  • 1 litre toned milk · 2 tbsp lemon juice in 2 tbsp water
  • 25 g nolen gur (or 25 g jaggery with 2 drops of rose water)
  • ¼ tsp cardamom powder · 5 g pistachios, finely chopped

Method

  1. Bring milk to a boil. Turn off the heat, wait 1 minute, then add the lemon water gradually, stirring, until the milk splits into curds and clear greenish whey.
  2. Pour into a muslin-lined colander; rinse under cold water to remove the lemon taste. Squeeze gently and hang 30 minutes — the chhena should be moist but not dripping (about 150 g).
  3. Knead the chhena on a plate with the heel of your palm for 5–6 minutes until completely smooth and slightly greasy.
  4. Mix in the gur. Cook in a non-stick pan on low, stirring, for 5–7 minutes until it leaves the sides and just holds together. Do not overcook or it turns grainy.
  5. Cool until warm, add cardamom, knead 1 minute, and shape into 9 small rounds. Press a little pistachio on top.

Protein optimisation

  • Chhena supplies 8.5 g per serving; pistachio 0.5 g.
  • Boost: knead in 15 g skimmed milk powder with the gur (+2 g protein, +20 kcal per serving).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: kacha golla — uncooked chhena with sugar, softer and rounder.
  • Substitution: if nolen gur is unavailable, use regular jaggery; the taste is earthier.
  • Storage: 2 days refrigerated.
  • Food safety: chhena is perishable; refrigerate within 2 hours.
  • Meal prep: make chhena a day ahead.
