Gatte ki Sabzi in Hung-Curd Gravy

LunchVegetarianGood sourceL13

Rajasthan's desert-kitchen classic: spiced besan dumplings boiled and simmered in a tangy curd gravy. Hung curd makes the gravy thicker and higher in protein, with no cream.

Ingredients

  • Gatte: 120 g besan · 30 g curd · 5 g oil · ½ tsp ajwain · ½ tsp red chilli powder · ¼ tsp turmeric · ½ tsp coriander powder · 3 g salt · about 2 tbsp water
  • Gravy: 150 g hung curd (from about 300 g curd) · 5 g besan · 10 g ghee or oil · 1 tsp cumin seeds · pinch of hing · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder · 1 tsp coriander powder · 1 tsp kasuri methi · 4 g salt · 250–300 ml gatte cooking water
  • Plate: 210 g whole-wheat atta for 6 rotis

Method

  1. Knead gatte ingredients into a stiff dough. Roll into 4 logs, 1.5 cm thick.
  2. Boil 1 litre water. Add the logs and boil 12–15 minutes, until they float and look slightly glossy. Drain, reserving the water. Cool and slice into 1.5 cm pieces.
  3. Whisk hung curd with besan, turmeric, chilli and coriander powders until smooth.
  4. Heat ghee; add cumin and hing. Lower the heat to the minimum and add the curd mixture, stirring constantly in one direction for 2 minutes so it does not split.
  5. Gradually add 250–300 ml of the reserved water, stirring. Simmer 5 minutes on low.
  6. Add gatte and salt; simmer 5–7 minutes until the gravy coats the pieces. Finish with kasuri methi.
  7. Serve with rotis.

Protein optimisation

  • Besan supplies 9 g per serving; hung curd 3 g.
  • Boost: add 50 g paneer cubes per serving to the gravy with the gatte (+9 g protein, +130 kcal, +10 g fat).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Gatte pulao — toss the boiled gatte with spiced rice instead of the gravy.
  • Substitution: Greek yogurt for hung curd.
  • Storage: 2 days refrigerated; reheat gently on low so the gravy does not split.
  • Food safety: refrigerate within 2 hours.
  • Meal prep: boil and refrigerate gatte up to 2 days ahead.
