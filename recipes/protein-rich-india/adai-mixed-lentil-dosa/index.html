Adai (Mixed-Lentil Dosa)

BreakfastVegetarianGood sourceB03

Adai is Tamil Nadu's thick, crisp dosa made from four dals and a little rice, with no fermentation — the most protein-rich of the South Indian dosas.

Ingredients

  • 80 g raw rice (idli or sona masoori) · 50 g chana dal · 50 g toor dal · 25 g urad dal · 25 g yellow moong dal
  • 3 dry red chillies · 10 g ginger · 10 curry leaves · ¼ tsp hing · 4 g salt (¾ tsp)
  • 60 g onion, finely chopped · 2 tbsp coriander
  • Cooking: 15 g groundnut oil (3 tsp) — gingelly (sesame) oil is traditional
  • Serving: 150 g curd per person, optional

Method

  1. Soak rice and all four dals together in 600 ml water for 3 hours. Drain.
  2. Grind coarsely with red chillies, ginger and 120–150 ml water. The batter should feel slightly grainy, like fine semolina, and be thicker than dosa batter.
  3. Stir in salt, hing, curry leaves, onion and coriander. Use immediately.
  4. Heat a tawa on medium. Spread 1 ladle (about 75 g) into an 18 cm circle, thicker than a dosa.
  5. Make a small hole in the centre with the ladle tip. Drizzle ¼ tsp oil in the hole and ¼ tsp around the edge.
  6. Cook 3 minutes until the underside is deep golden. Flip and cook 2 minutes more.
  7. Serve hot with curd, avial or coconut chutney.

Protein optimisation

  • Dals supply about 11 g per serving; rice about 2 g. Combining four dals with rice provides complementary amino acids.
  • Boost: serve with 150 g curd — adds 5 g protein and 90 kcal. Alternatively, swap 30 g rice for 30 g more toor dal (+1.5 g protein per serving, slightly softer adai).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Kerala-style — add 2 tbsp grated coconut to the batter (adds about 25 kcal and 2 g saturated fat per serving).
  • Substitution: any mix of dals works if chana dal stays at least a third of the total, for crispness.
  • Storage: batter keeps 24 hours refrigerated; it may begin to sour.
  • Food safety: do not leave unfermented batter at room temperature for more than 4 hours in hot weather.
  • Meal prep: grind the night before; add onions in the morning.
