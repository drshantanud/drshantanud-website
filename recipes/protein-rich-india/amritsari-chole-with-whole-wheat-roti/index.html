Amritsari Chole with Whole-Wheat Roti

LunchVegetarianGood sourceL02

Amritsar's dark, tangy chole gets its colour from tea and its sourness from dried pomegranate seed; the chickpeas stay whole but meltingly soft.

Ingredients

  • 180 g kabuli chana, dry · 1 black tea bag (or 1 tsp tea leaves tied in muslin) · ½ tsp baking soda (optional, for softness)
  • 120 g onion, finely chopped · 150 g tomato, chopped · 15 g ginger, julienned · 2 green chillies, slit
  • 15 g groundnut or mustard oil · 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 10 g chole masala · 5 g anardana powder · ½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder · 5 g salt
  • Plate: 210 g whole-wheat atta for 6 rotis · 300 g onion-cucumber salad · 300 g curd

Method

  1. Soak chana in 1 litre water for 8 hours. Drain.
  2. Pressure-cook with 750 ml water, the tea bag, salt and baking soda (if using) for 1 whistle on high and 20 minutes on low, until a chickpea crushes easily. Discard the tea bag; keep the dark liquid.
  3. Heat oil; add cumin, then onion. Cook 8 minutes until browned.
  4. Add half the ginger and the chillies for 1 minute. Add tomato, chole masala, anardana and chilli powder. Cook 8 minutes until thick and dark, mashing the tomato.
  5. Add chana and its liquid. Simmer 15 minutes uncovered, pressing a few chickpeas to thicken.
  6. Finish with the remaining ginger. Serve with roti, salad and curd.

Protein optimisation

  • Chickpeas supply 11 g per serving; rotis 8 g; curd 3.5 g.
  • Boost: stir 50 g paneer cubes per serving into the chole for the last 5 minutes (+9 g protein, +130 kcal, +10 g fat).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Pindi chole — omit tomato; use more anardana and dry spices for a drier, darker dish.
  • Substitution: canned chickpeas (drained, rinsed; about 450 g) save the soaking and pressure-cooking.
  • Traditional pairing: kulcha or bhatura; whole-wheat roti keeps the plate lighter.
  • Storage: 3 days refrigerated; freezes 2 months.
  • Food safety: reheat until boiling throughout.
  • Meal prep: excellent; flavour improves on day 2.
