Moong Dal Paddu

SnackVegetarianHigh proteinS12

Crisp-outside, soft-inside dumplings cooked in Karnataka's paddu (paniyaram) pan, made from a moong-dominant batter so they are higher in protein than the usual rice-urad version.

Ingredients

  • 110 g yellow moong dal · 25 g urad dal · 25 g raw rice
  • 50 g curd · ¼ tsp baking soda · 3 g salt
  • 50 g onion and 40 g carrot, finely chopped · 8 curry leaves · 1 green chilli · 1 tsp grated ginger · 2 tbsp coriander
  • 10 g groundnut oil (2 tsp) for the pan

Method

  1. Soak dals and rice together for 3 hours. Drain.
  2. Grind with curd and 50–60 ml water to a thick batter that drops from a spoon in a ribbon. Rest 30 minutes.
  3. Stir in salt, vegetables, curry leaves, chilli, ginger, coriander and, just before cooking, the baking soda.
  4. Heat the paddu pan on medium. Put a few drops of oil in each cavity.
  5. Fill each cavity three-quarters full. Cover and cook 3 minutes until the base is golden.
  6. Turn each paddu with a skewer; cook 2 minutes more, uncovered, until golden all over.
  7. Serve hot with coconut or tomato chutney.

Protein optimisation

  • Moong dal supplies 9 g per serving, urad 2 g, rice and curd 1 g.
  • Boost: serve with peanut chutney (20 g peanuts per serving: +5 g protein, +115 kcal).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Chettinad kuzhi paniyaram — add 1 tsp chana dal to the tempering and a pinch of crushed pepper.
  • Substitution: use leftover B13 ragi-moong dosa batter.
  • Storage: batter keeps 24 hours refrigerated; cooked paddu are best warm, 1 day refrigerated.
  • Food safety: refrigerate batter if not used within 4 hours.
  • Meal prep: good.
