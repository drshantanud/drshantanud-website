Palak Paneer, Lighter

DinnerVegetarianHigh proteinD01

The Punjabi classic without cream: a bright, garlicky spinach purée with soft paneer, enriched with a little milk. The spinach carries the flavour, so nothing is lost.

Ingredients

  • 200 g full-fat paneer, in 2 cm cubes · 500 g spinach, washed
  • 100 g onion, finely chopped · 80 g tomato · 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste · 2 green chillies · 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 10 g groundnut oil · 1 tsp cumin seeds · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 tsp coriander powder · ½ tsp garam masala · 1 tsp kasuri methi · 4 g salt · 60 ml milk
  • Plate: 105 g whole-wheat atta for 3 rotis · 300 g cucumber-onion salad

Method

  1. Blanch spinach in boiling water for 1 minute; plunge into iced water. Squeeze lightly and blend with chillies to a smooth purée.
  2. Heat oil; add cumin and sliced garlic until golden. Add onion; cook 5 minutes until soft.
  3. Add ginger-garlic paste and tomato; cook 4 minutes until the tomato breaks down. Add turmeric and coriander powder.
  4. Add the spinach purée, salt and 80 ml water. Simmer 3–4 minutes only, to keep the colour bright.
  5. Stir in milk, then paneer, garam masala and crushed kasuri methi. Simmer 2 minutes. Serve with roti and salad.

Protein optimisation

  • Paneer supplies 12 g per serving; spinach 3.5 g; milk and other vegetables 1.5 g.
  • Boost: replace milk with 60 g hung curd whisked in off the heat (+1 g protein per serving), or add 50 g more paneer (+3 g protein, +45 kcal, +3.5 g fat per serving).
  • Low-fat paneer (check the label) saves about 70 kcal and 8 g fat per serving.

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Punjabi winter saag paneer — use 250 g mustard greens and 250 g spinach, and a little makki atta to thicken.
  • Substitution: firm tofu for paneer (see L16).
  • Storage: 2 days refrigerated; the colour dulls on reheating.
  • Food safety: refrigerate within 2 hours.
  • Meal prep: make the spinach purée ahead; it keeps 1 day.
