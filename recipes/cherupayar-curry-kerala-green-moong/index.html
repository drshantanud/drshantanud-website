Cherupayar Curry (Kerala Green Moong)

DinnerVegetarianGood sourceD10

Whole green moong cooked soft in a coconut-shallot gravy with curry leaves — a Kerala staple, traditionally eaten with kanji (rice gruel) for a light evening meal.

Ingredients

  • 180 g whole green moong (cherupayar)
  • 30 g grated coconut, ground with ½ tsp cumin, 2 green chillies and 2 garlic cloves
  • 50 g shallots, sliced · ½ tsp turmeric · 5 g salt
  • Tempering: 8 g coconut oil · ½ tsp mustard seeds · 2 dry red chillies · 12 curry leaves
  • Plate: 120 g Kerala matta (red) rice · moru (300 g curd whisked with 300 ml water, ginger, green chilli and salt)

Method

  1. Soak moong 4 hours. Pressure-cook with turmeric and 600 ml water for 3 whistles until soft.
  2. Add the coconut paste and salt; simmer 5 minutes, mashing a few beans to thicken.
  3. For kanji, cook matta rice with 1 litre water and salt until very soft (pressure-cook 6 whistles); serve with its cooking water.
  4. Heat coconut oil; add mustard seeds, red chillies, curry leaves and shallots. Fry until the shallots are golden; pour over the curry.
  5. Serve the curry with kanji and moru.

Protein optimisation

  • Green moong supplies 14 g per serving; rice 3 g and moru 3.5 g on the plate.
  • Boost: use 200 g curd per person for the moru (+3.5 g protein, +60 kcal).
  • Lighter: groundnut oil and 20 g coconut save about 3 g saturated fat per serving.

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Kerala cherupayar thoran — a dry stir-fry of cooked moong with grated coconut; serve as a side.
  • Substitution: 2 whole-wheat chapatis instead of kanji.
  • Storage: 3 days refrigerated.
  • Food safety: reheat until boiling; keep moru chilled.
  • Meal prep: excellent.
