Ragi-Moong Dosa

BreakfastVegetarianHigh proteinB13

A no-ferment Karnataka-style dosa that pairs moong dal with ragi: moong carries the protein, ragi adds earthy flavour, colour and calcium.

Ingredients

  • 120 g yellow moong dal, dry · 40 g ragi flour
  • 10 g ginger · 2 green chillies · ½ tsp cumin · pinch of hing · 3 g salt
  • 40 g onion, finely chopped · 10 curry leaves, chopped · 2 tbsp coriander
  • 10 g groundnut oil (2 tsp)

Method

  1. Soak moong dal 3 hours; drain.
  2. Grind with ginger, chillies, cumin and 100 ml water until smooth.
  3. Whisk in ragi flour, hing and salt. Add 40–60 ml water to reach a pourable dosa batter. Rest 10 minutes so the ragi hydrates.
  4. Stir in onion, curry leaves and coriander.
  5. Heat a tawa on medium-high. Pour 1 ladle (about 70 g) and spread to an 18 cm circle.
  6. Drizzle ½ tsp oil around the edge. Cook 2 minutes until the edges darken and lift; flip and cook 1 minute.
  7. Serve with coconut or peanut chutney.

Protein optimisation

  • Moong dal supplies about 14.5 g per serving; ragi 1.5 g.
  • Boost: serve with 25 g peanut chutney per serving (+6 g protein, +145 kcal, +12 g fat) or 150 g curd (+5 g protein, +90 kcal).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Mangalorean touch — add 2 tbsp grated coconut and 1 tbsp chopped onion to the batter (adds about 25 kcal and 2 g saturated fat per serving).
  • Substitution: jowar flour in place of ragi gives a lighter-coloured dosa.
  • Storage: batter keeps 24 hours refrigerated.
  • Food safety: as for any unfermented batter, refrigerate if not used within 4 hours.
  • Meal prep: grind the night before.
