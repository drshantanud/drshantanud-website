Pithla with Jowar Bhakri

DinnerVegetarianGood sourceD15

Maharashtra's fast, comforting besan curry, cooked with garlic, green chilli and onion to a soft, spoonable consistency, with bhakri and curd — a farmhouse supper.

Ingredients

  • 120 g besan · about 550 ml water
  • 100 g onion, finely chopped · 6 garlic cloves, crushed · 2–3 green chillies · 10 curry leaves · 2 tbsp coriander
  • 12 g groundnut oil · ½ tsp mustard seeds · ½ tsp cumin · pinch of hing · ¼ tsp turmeric · 5 g salt
  • Plate: 180 g jowar flour for 3 bhakri (method in L04) · 450 g curd

Method

  1. Whisk besan with 450 ml water, turmeric and salt until completely smooth.
  2. Heat oil; add mustard, cumin, hing, garlic, chillies and curry leaves until the garlic turns golden. Add onion; cook 3 minutes.
  3. Lower the heat. Pour in the besan mixture, stirring constantly to prevent lumps.
  4. Cook 6–8 minutes, stirring, adding up to 100 ml more water, until it thickens to a soft, spoonable consistency and no longer tastes raw. Cover and steam on low for 2 minutes.
  5. Garnish with coriander. Serve hot with bhakri and curd.

Protein optimisation

  • Besan supplies 8.5 g per serving; bhakri 6 g and curd 5 g on the plate.
  • Boost: crumble 50 g paneer into the pithla at the end (+9 g protein, +130 kcal per serving), or serve with 150 g hung curd instead of curd (+4 g protein, +50 kcal).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: zunka — a dry version with less water, cooked until crumbly; serve with thecha (green-chilli garlic chutney).
  • Substitution: whole-wheat chapati or rice bhakri.
  • Storage: best fresh; it sets as it cools. Loosen with hot water.
  • Food safety: refrigerate within 2 hours.
  • Meal prep: chop onion and garlic ahead.
