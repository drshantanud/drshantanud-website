Foxtail Millet and Moong Pongal

BreakfastVegetarianGood sourceB20

Tamil Nadu's comforting ven pongal, made with foxtail millet instead of rice and an equal weight of moong dal, seasoned with black pepper, cumin and ghee.

Ingredients

  • 70 g foxtail millet · 70 g yellow moong dal
  • 600 ml water · 3 g salt
  • 10 g ghee (2 tsp) · 1 tsp black peppercorns, lightly crushed · 1 tsp cumin seeds · 1 tsp grated ginger · 10 curry leaves · pinch of hing · 10 g cashews
  • Serving: coconut chutney or 150 g curd per person

Method

  1. Dry-roast moong dal on medium-low for 3–4 minutes until it smells nutty and turns a shade darker.
  2. Rinse the dal and millet together. Soak 15 minutes (optional; it gives a softer pongal).
  3. Pressure-cook with 600 ml water and salt for 4 whistles. Let pressure release naturally.
  4. Mash lightly with a ladle until creamy. Add up to 100 ml hot water if it is too thick — pongal should be soft and spoonable.
  5. Heat ghee in a small pan. Fry cashews until golden; add pepper, cumin, ginger, curry leaves and hing for 30 seconds.
  6. Pour the tempering over the pongal and stir. Serve hot.

Protein optimisation

  • Moong dal supplies 8.5 g per serving, foxtail millet 4 g, cashews 1 g.
  • Boost: serve with 150 g curd as above, or with sambar made with 30 g toor dal per serving (+6.5 g protein, +110 kcal).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: temple-style — add 5 g more ghee and a pinch of crushed pepper on top (adds 45 kcal and 3 g saturated fat).
  • Substitution: little millet (samai) or rice in place of foxtail millet.
  • Storage: 2 days refrigerated; reheat with hot water.
  • Food safety: cool within 1 hour and refrigerate; reheat until steaming.
  • Meal prep: good.
