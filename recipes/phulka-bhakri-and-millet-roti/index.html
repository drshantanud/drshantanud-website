Phulka, Bhakri and Millet Roti

RotiVegetarianE12

How to make the rotis in the diet plan at the size the calories assume: a 15 cm phulka from 25 g atta, an 18 cm bhakri from 40 g jowar or bajra flour, and a 15 cm ragi roti from 35 g flour.

Ingredients

  • Phulka (makes 6): 150 g whole-wheat atta · about 95 ml water · pinch of salt
  • Jowar or bajra bhakri (makes 4): 160 g jowar or bajra flour · about 130 ml hot water · pinch of salt
  • Ragi roti (makes 4): 140 g ragi flour · about 120 ml boiling water · pinch of salt
  • No oil or ghee is included; each ½ tsp ghee adds about 22 kcal

Method

  1. Phulka: knead atta and water to a soft dough and rest 15 minutes. Divide into 6 balls of about 40 g (25 g atta each). Roll thin to 15 cm (6 in). Cook on a hot tawa 30 seconds, flip, cook 30 seconds more, then place directly on the flame with tongs until it puffs.
  2. Bhakri: mix flour and salt with hot water and knead each portion just before rolling (millet dough dries quickly). Pat a 40 g ball of flour, as dough, into an 18 cm (7 in) circle on a floured board or between sheets. Cook on a hot tawa, brush the top with water, flip after 1 minute, then roast on the flame or tawa until spotted.
  3. Ragi roti: pour boiling water over the flour, stir and rest 5 minutes, then knead. Roll 35 g flour portions to 15 cm between sheets and cook like bhakri.

Protein optimisation

  • A phulka gives about 2.6 g protein, a jowar bhakri 4 g, a bajra bhakri 4.4 g and a ragi roti 2.5 g.
  • Protein comes mainly from the dal, paneer, curd or eggs served alongside.

Variations, serving and storage

  • Size matters: a 20 cm roti from 35 g atta has about 40% more calories than the 15 cm phulka.
  • Multigrain: mix atta with 25% jowar or besan for more fibre.
  • Storage: phulkas are best fresh; bhakri keeps a day wrapped in a cloth.
