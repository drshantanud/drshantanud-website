Matimah (Whole Black Urad) with Rice

LunchVegetarianGood sourceL20

Assam's simple, earthy black urad dal, cooked with ginger and finished with a mustard-oil tempering — a staple of the everyday Assamese meal of rice, dal and greens.

Ingredients

  • 180 g whole black urad (matimah), dry
  • 15 g ginger, julienned · 10 g garlic · 2 green chillies · 100 g tomato (optional; some homes add it, others don't) · ½ tsp turmeric · 5 g salt
  • Tempering: 10 g mustard oil · 1 bay leaf · ½ tsp cumin or panch phoron · 2 dry red chillies
  • Plate: 180 g raw rice · 300 g lai xaak (mustard greens) or other greens, sautéed in 9 g mustard oil

Method

  1. Soak urad 6 hours. Drain.
  2. Pressure-cook with turmeric, ginger, chillies and 700 ml water for 1 whistle on high, then 20 minutes on low, until the beans are soft and partly broken.
  3. Mash a few beans to thicken. Add salt, tomato (if using) and 150 ml water; simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Heat mustard oil until it just smokes; lower the heat. Add bay leaf, cumin, red chillies and garlic until golden. Pour over the dal.
  5. Serve with rice and greens.

Protein optimisation

  • Whole urad supplies 14 g per serving; rice 4 g; greens 2 g.
  • Boost: serve with 100 g curd (+3.5 g protein, +60 kcal), as in many Assamese homes.

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: Matimah with khar — add 1 tbsp khar (an alkaline filtrate from banana peel ash), traditional in Assam, which gives a distinctive flavour.
  • Substitution: whole masoor dal cooks faster (25 minutes) with a similar texture.
  • Storage: 3 days refrigerated; freezes 2 months.
  • Food safety: reheat until boiling.
  • Meal prep: excellent.
