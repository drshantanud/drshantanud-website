Bhapa Doi

DessertVegetarianGood sourceDS2

Bengal's steamed sweet yogurt — silky, set like a custard — made with hung curd, milk and milk powder instead of condensed milk, and sweetened with nolen gur or jaggery.

Good source (just under 12 g). The boost below — 100 g more hung curd in place of the milk — brings it to about 12.5 g and makes it High protein. A condensed-milk bhapa doi typically has 20–25 g added sugar per ramekin. Values checked against IFCT 2017 on 15 Oct 2026.

Ingredients

  • 250 g hung curd (from about 550 g curd, strained 4 hours)
  • 100 ml toned milk · 40 g skimmed milk powder
  • 30 g nolen gur (date-palm jaggery) or regular jaggery, grated
  • ¼ tsp cardamom powder · 5 g pistachios, slivered

Method

  1. Warm the milk and dissolve the gur in it. Cool to room temperature (hot milk would split the curd).
  2. Whisk hung curd until smooth. Add the milk powder and gur-milk; whisk until no lumps remain. Stir in cardamom.
  3. Pour into 3 small ramekins or steel katoris. Cover each tightly with foil.
  4. Steam over gently simmering water for 20–25 minutes until set at the edges with a slight wobble in the centre. (Or bake in a water bath at 160 °C for 25–30 minutes.)
  5. Cool, then chill at least 2 hours. Garnish with pistachios.

Protein optimisation

  • Hung curd supplies 5 g per serving, milk powder 4.5 g, milk 1 g.
  • Boost: replace the 100 ml milk with 100 g more hung curd (+1 g protein, +15 kcal per serving; denser texture; makes it High protein).

Variations, serving and storage

  • Regional: mishti doi — set sweetened milk with a culture overnight; caramelised sugar gives its colour (higher in sugar).
  • Substitution: Greek yogurt for hung curd.
  • Storage: 3 days refrigerated, covered.
  • Food safety: chill within 1 hour of steaming.
  • Meal prep: excellent — make a day ahead.
