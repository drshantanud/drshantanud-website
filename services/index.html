Clinical Services

Heart procedures and preventive care, explained in plain language. Your doctor will advise which treatment is suitable for you.

Coronary Interventions

Complex angioplasty

Angioplasty and stenting for blockages that are long, calcified or in difficult locations.

CTO PCI

Opening an artery that has been completely blocked for a long time (chronic total occlusion).

Left main PCI

Stenting the left main artery, the large vessel that supplies most of the heart muscle.

Bifurcation PCI

Treating blockages at the point where a coronary artery divides into two branches.

Rotablation

A tiny rotating burr breaks up hard calcium so that a stent can expand fully.

IVUS / OCT guided PCI

Imaging from inside the artery to measure the blockage and check the stent.

Drug-coated balloon procedures

A balloon coated with medicine that treats selected blockages without leaving a new stent.

Structural Heart

TAVI

Replacing a narrowed aortic valve through a catheter, without open-heart surgery, in selected patients.

Valvular interventions

Catheter-based treatment of suitable narrowed heart valves.

Structural heart procedures

Device closure of certain holes in the heart, such as ASD and PDA.

Other Services

Frequently Asked Questions

General answers for patients. They are not a substitute for advice from your own doctor.

What is angioplasty?

Angioplasty (PCI) is a procedure to open a narrowed or blocked heart artery. A thin tube is passed through a blood vessel, usually at the wrist or groin, and a balloon is inflated at the blockage. A small mesh tube called a stent is usually left in place to keep the artery open.

Is angioplasty painful?

It is usually done with local anaesthesia and light sedation. You are awake but comfortable. Most people feel pressure or a brief warm sensation, and some feel mild chest discomfort when the balloon is inflated. Your team will explain what to expect.

How long does recovery take after angioplasty?

Many people go home within one to two days and return to light activity within about a week, but this depends on the reason for the procedure and your overall health. Your doctor will advise on medicines, activity and follow-up.

What is a CTO (chronic total occlusion) and can it be opened?

A CTO is an artery that has been completely blocked for a long time. Some can be opened with specialised wires and techniques. Whether it is worth attempting depends on your symptoms, scans and the anatomy, so this is decided after a careful assessment.

What is rotablation and why is it needed?

Rotablation uses a tiny, fast-spinning burr to break up hard calcium inside an artery. This lets a balloon and stent expand properly in heavily calcified blockages.

What are IVUS and OCT?

IVUS (ultrasound) and OCT (light-based imaging) are imaging methods used from inside the artery. They show the size of the blockage and help check that the stent has been placed and expanded well.

What is TAVI?

TAVI (transcatheter aortic valve implantation) replaces a narrowed aortic valve through a catheter, usually from an artery in the leg, without open-heart surgery. It is considered for selected patients with severe aortic stenosis after a heart team assessment.

How do I know which treatment is right for me?

The best treatment depends on your symptoms, test results, other medical conditions and preferences. Medicines, angioplasty and bypass surgery are all options in different situations. Book a consultation so your doctor can assess you personally.
